Journal article · 2025

1. Critical Realism, Local Knowledge, and Autonomous Knowledge Systems in Economics: An Agent-based Modeling Investigation

Zhixuan Feng* and Zhaoyu Chen

Political Economy Quarterly, No. 2, 2025 (in Chinese).

Abstract and keywords

This paper aims to adopt a relatively formalized approach to explore the potential advantages of autonomous knowledge systems within the field of economics compared to alternative epistemological frameworks. Drawing upon theoretical perspectives from Critical Realism, Social Positioning Theory, and Local Knowledge Paradigms, we construct a multi-layered behavioral logic embedded in an Agent-based Model. Employing this computational model, we systematically analyze the relationship between autonomous knowledge systems and other epistemological approaches—including universalist knowledge systems—in terms of their respective capacities for economic explanation and prediction under varying social ontological assumptions. The study finds that when economic systems manifest any of the following conditions: (1) macro-structural influences on agent behavior, (2) socially embedded relational configurations, or (3) complex networked interactions among individual actors, autonomous knowledge systems consistently exhibit superior explanatory and predictive power. These findings provide theoretically generalizable support for the systematic construction of autonomous knowledge frameworks in economic research.

Keywords: autonomous knowledge systems; Agent-based model; critical realism; social positioning theory; local knowledge paradigm

Forthcoming

2. Competition Theory in Political Economy and Its Implications for Governing “Involutionary” Competition

Zhixuan Feng and Zhaoyu Chen*

Accepted, forthcoming in Journal of Renmin University of China (in Chinese).

Abstract and keywords

“Involutionary” competition is a pressing problem facing the China’s economy. Grounded in Marxist competition theory and drawing on evolutionary economics’ analysis of firm behavior, this article develops an analytical framework comprising the general determinations of competition, the institutional environment, and competing firms. The framework posits that market competition has a dual character: it promotes accumulation while also inducing overaccumulation. Its concrete outcomes depend on whether competition, accumulation, and profits can form a virtuous circle. As capital accumulation deepens and the economic structure changes, the existing institutional system becomes less capable of supporting the processes of competition and accumulation. Meanwhile, polarization in firm size and the path-dependent lock-in of firm capabilities impede firms’ timely adaptation to the new competitive environment, causing market competition to evolve into “involutionary” competition. Governing “involutionary” competition therefore requires reshaping the institutional system that sustains a virtuous circle among competition, accumulation, and profits, enabling state-owned enterprises to play a greater role in improving the competitive environment, and promoting the continuous renewal of firms’ knowledge and capabilities.

Keywords: “Involutionary” competition; Marxist competition theory; Capital accumulation; Competitive regime; Firm capabilities

Book chapter

3. Value Determination and Regional Labor Productivity Differences Measurement under Marx-Sraffa Framework

Zhixuan Feng and Zhaoyu Chen

In Rong Zhaozhi and Li Bangxi (eds.), Total Factor Productivity in Marxism: Method and Algorithms (in Chinese).

Abstract and keywords

This paper investigates the value determination process under multi-technology conditions within the framework of Marxist political economy, and develops a methodology for measuring inter-regional labor productivity disparities. Employing the Marx-Sraffa multi-technology linear production model with Chinese regional input-output table data from 2007 to 2017, the study compares two distinct value determination methods and calculates labor productivity differences across Chinese regions. Key findings include: Firstly, regarding value determination, although theoretical differences exist between the Murata method and CFFFV method, their practical applications demonstrate minimal divergence with fundamentally consistent conclusions. Secondly, China's inter-regional disparities in per capita income are primarily determined by labor productivity differentials, while value transfer also exerts a measurable influence on regional income gaps. Thirdly, Regional samples can be categorized into four typologies based on labor productivity and value transfer differentials, each facing distinct developmental challenges.

Keywords: Marx-Sraffa Framework; Multi-technology Linear Production Model; the Determination of value; Regional Labor Productivity Differences
